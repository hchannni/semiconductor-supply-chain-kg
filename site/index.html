Who supplies, makes, uses and owns what in the chip industry. Relations were extracted by LLMs from the Wikipedia articles and SEC annual filings of 50 semiconductor and AI-hardware companies, then matched to canonical entities, merged across sources and checked against the CSET ETO Chip Explorer.

    Relations

    Confidence: HIGH when at least two independent publishers state the relation (all filings of one company count as one publisher, Wikipedia as another), MEDIUM for a single publisher, LOW for unresolved conflicts and relations that only held in the past or for a limited scope. Support (0–1) adds up the evidence from independent publishers. It ranks relations; it is not a probability. Click a row to read the evidence.

    Limits of these numbers

    • Version 2 is the reviewed graph: 143 flagged error cases were checked against the sources, 50 claims corrected and 117 set aside, and 244 missing evidence statements recovered. The result has 2,988 relations instead of 3,140. The review was model-assisted, not expert gold.
    • The precision gain (76% to 81%) is measured after fixing errors that earlier checks had found, partly on the same sampled relations, so it is not a held-out test of the method.
    • The pairwise ETO judge finds every hand-labelled exact match (17 of 17) but also accepts 7 pairs that people marked as only partial, so true strict recall is likely lower, around 40%. Recall against ETO did not change between versions.
    • ETO's reference is mostly equipment makers (104 of 128 pairs are tools) and has no rows for fabless and analog companies, so recall describes a narrow slice of the graph. Three companies hold 72 of the 128 pairs, which is why the intervals are wide.
    • The support score ranks relations but is not calibrated. The HIGH / MEDIUM label is the better signal.
